# Reflection: Time Perception

Do you think an average human life is around 70 years? So that would mean the middle point of our life is 35 years old.

But I think that might be a wrong idea, because for 35 to actually feel like the midpoint it needs to assume that our perception of time is linear, but psychologically time does not necessarily feel linear. As we accumulate more years, one additional year becomes a smaller proportion of everything we've already experienced.

You might have noticed that time is flying by fast recently, like it's already going to be September of 2026 and to me it feels like I have celebrated the New Year of 2026 just a month ago.
This happens because our perception of time is kind of logarithmic.

For a newborn, its first year would be full of new experiences, but as the child grows, the amount of years they have already lived gets longer and longer compared to that first one year.
For example, when you're young, one year is a huge percentage of your entire life, but as you get older, that same one year becomes a smaller percentage of everything you've already experienced

And maybe that's also why time feels like it's moving faster. Which is why I think it's very important to try new things and always have a change in your day to day life.

Go try out a new skill, go on a trip, a hike, go to a different place and experience new cultures, make new friends, get in shape.
Because when you do new things, you create more memories and when you look back at that time, there is actually more there to remember instead of everything just blending together.

For these are the true ways of making your perception of time slower.
